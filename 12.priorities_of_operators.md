## **Operators and their priorities**

Consider the following expression:

**`2 + 3 * 5`**

-  We should first multiply 3 by 5 and, keeping the 15 in your memory, then add it to 2, thus getting the result of 17.

- Most of Python's operators have left-sided binding, which means that   the calculation of the expression is conducted from left to right.

- This simple example will show you how it works. Take a look


```python
print(9 % 6 % 2)
```
___
### Exponentiation `**`

Right‑sided binding → evaluated right to left.
```python
print(2 ** 2 ** 3)
```
`2 ** (2 ** 3)   # 2 ** 8 = 256`

<H4>🔑 Key Takeaways<H4>

Most operators (+, -, *, /, //, %) → left‑sided binding.

Exponentiation (**) → right‑sided binding.

Parentheses can always be used to make the order explicit.
___

## 📊 Priority Order (highest → lowest)
1. `**` (exponentiation) → **right‑sided binding**  
   Example: `2 ** 2 ** 3 = 256`
2. Unary `+` and `-`  
   Example: `4 ** -1 = 0.25`
3. `*`, `/`, `%`
4. Binary `+`, `-`

👉 Parentheses always evaluated first.  
Example: `15 - 1 * (5 * (1 + 2)) = 0`
